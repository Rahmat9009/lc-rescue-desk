> typography recommendation (now Source Serif 4 + Source Sans 3 + IBM Plex Mono), the brand colours
> (now `#023527` / `#478f81`, sampled from the supplied logo), the spacing scale, the 15px/16px body

> than written. Read it alongside this file.

Rendered specimen: `studies/type-specimen.png` (source `studies/type-specimen.html`). Both alternatives are set with
**identical LC Rescue content** at identical sizes, and both were rendered in a real browser with the webfonts
actually loaded — `document.fonts` confirmed `Source Serif 4`, `Archivo`, `IBM Plex Mono` all resolved. Nothing below
is judged from a font's reputation.

Neither alternative uses a third family. Monospace is counted as one of the two and is confined to content where
character-level fidelity or column alignment is the point.

---

## Alternative A — "Instrument of record"
**Source Serif 4** (display, headings, all prose) + **IBM Plex Mono** (document text, quotations, dates, amounts, labels, buttons)

Hierarchy is carried by a **category** difference: anything the product *says* is serif; anything a document *states*
or the system *labels* is mono. A reader can tell narration from evidence without reading a word.

## Alternative B — "Operations console"
**Archivo** (display, headings, all prose, UI chrome) + **IBM Plex Mono** (document text, quotations, dates, amounts)

A single well-tuned grotesque does all the talking; hierarchy comes from size, weight and space alone. Monospace
appears only inside document content.

## Critique, against Impeccable's `typeset` criteria

Impeccable's guidance states that for **Operate** surfaces "stability, scanability, and measure come first. A single
well-tuned family and fixed role scale are often right," and warns specifically about roles whose "adjacent sizes or
weights are too close to carry different jobs." It also sets a 45–75ch measure and a 16px body floor. Judged on that:

### Authority and fit
- **A** — Source Serif 4 is a text-first transitional with moderate contrast and sturdy bracketed serifs. At 52px/400
  it has institutional authority without the high-contrast Didone glamour that currently signals "AI landing page".
  It fits a document-of-record product literally: letters of credit *are* documents, and the face reads like one.
  **Strong fit.**
- **B** — Archivo at 600/-0.032em is confident and dense, but a tight bold grotesque hero is the most-produced hero
  in software right now. It is well-made and it is also exactly the register the brief called "generic".
  **Competent, not distinctive.**

### Hierarchy
- **A** — Three roles are unmistakable at a glance in the specimen: serif prose, mono data, mono uppercase label.
  The label/heading confusion risk is structurally impossible because they are different families. **Strong.**
- **B** — Label (Archivo 600, 12px, uppercase, tracked) and card title (Archivo 600, 17px) differ only by size and
  case. This is precisely the "too close to carry different jobs" failure Impeccable names. Workable, but it needs
  colour to do work that type should be doing. **Weaker.**

### Scale and consistency
Equal. Both use the same named role scale in `studies/tokens.css`; no size in it is unused or arbitrary.

### Reading
- **A** — body at 17px/1.62 measured ~62ch in the specimen. Source Serif's large x-height keeps it comfortable, and
  its lining figures sit near cap height so `2026-10-16` inside a sentence does not jump. The four-sentence
  unresolved-questions paragraph — the longest prose in the product — reads notably better in A than in B.
- **B** — 16px/1.65, ~70ch. Clean and neutral. Slightly flatter over four sentences of hedged legal prose.
- Both clear the 16px floor. **The current production build does not: body is 15px.** Either alternative fixes it.

### Stress
- **A** — the serif tolerates long headings well; at 330px the 19px heading and 15px body hold (verified in the
  specimen's small-screen panel). Fallback is Georgia, which is metrically close enough that a failed webfont
  degrades rather than breaks.
- **B** — excellent at small sizes, marginally more compact. Fallback to `system-ui` is near-seamless.
- **B is slightly more robust under stress; A is adequate.**

### Delivery
Equal risk, and both carry one. Each adds two webfont families to a Cloudflare Worker that currently ships none.
Both must use `font-display:swap` and a metric-aware fallback. A's variable `opsz 8..60` axis is the one real extra
cost; pinning the loaded range rather than shipping the full axis mitigates it.

### Numerals — decisive for this product
This interface is mostly dates, amounts, versions and line numbers. Both alternatives set all of them in IBM Plex
Mono with `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`, so `2026-10-12` above `2026-10-16` aligns digit-for-digit in the
`DeadlineStrip` key/value rows. Verified in the specimen. **No difference between the alternatives — and it is the
single most valuable typographic decision in the system, which is why mono is non-negotiable in both.**

### The one thing I got wrong in the first pass
In the specimen, A's buttons are set in Source Serif 4 600, and they read soft and bookish — wrong for an action
that binds document versions. I rebuilt it: **A's buttons use IBM Plex Mono 500**, joining the label role rather
than the prose role. In `studies/study-1-hero.png` and `study-2-latest-action.png` this reads precise and
system-like, and it resolves A's only material weakness without adding a family.

## Rejected, with reasons

- **Reducto's one-word-in-monospace headline** ("Turn documents into `data`.") — observed and genuinely clever,
  because the type switch *encodes* the prose→structured-data transformation. Rejected anyway: the supplied LC
  Rescue headline has no single word carrying that meaning, so applying it would be the "accent one word" tic with
  no semantic justification.
- **Keeping Libre Franklin** (the current build) — it is a decent Franklin Gothic revival, but it is doing the same
  job as Archivo while being the incumbent that the brief judged generic. No reason to keep it over either option.
- **Inter, Geist, Söhne-alikes** — Inter is the default of defaults; the others add licensing or availability risk
  for no gain here.
- **A third family** (serif display + sans UI + mono, the Harvey arrangement literally) — over the two-family budget,
  and A's serif/mono split achieves the same role separation with one fewer family.

---

## Recommendation: **Alternative A**, with mono buttons and labels

**Why.** The brief's complaint was that the redesign "still feels generic", and B is the better-behaved but more
generic option. A is distinctive without being eccentric, and — more importantly — its distinctiveness is *about the
subject*: a product whose entire claim is "every value is tied to the line it came from" gains a typographic system
where narration and evidence are visibly different categories. A also supplies the coherence mechanism the brief
asks for and that Harvey demonstrates: the serif that sets the hero headline also sets the case title and the
recommendation in the workspace, so the two surfaces are visibly one product.

**Tradeoffs, honestly.**
1. **Serif body is the contested call.** Serif prose in a dense operations UI is unusual, and if the eventual
   workspace becomes much denser than today's, A will come under pressure where B would not. The mitigation is
   already in the spec: prose is capped at 62ch and confined to the recommendation, reasons and unresolved lists —
   every dense region (findings, key/values, history chips) is mono or short sans-free labels.
2. **A is less forgiving of a webfont failure.** Georgia is a reasonable fallback but noticeably wider than Source
   Serif 4, so a failed load reflows the hero more than B's fallback would.
3. **Slightly higher payload** from the optical-size axis.
4. **B remains the correct choice if the product's direction changes** toward a high-density multi-case queue or a
   table-heavy console. A is right for the product as it actually is today: a small number of cases, read carefully,
   with long hedged prose that must be read accurately.

**Not recommended either way:** keeping the 15px body. Both alternatives move it to 16–17px.
